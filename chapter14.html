D. Answer the Following Questions in Brief

1. Distinguish between asexual and sexual reproduction.

Answer: Asexual reproduction involves only a single parent and no gametes are formed, whereas sexual reproduction involves two parents and the fusion of male and female gametes takes place.

2. How is reproduction carried out in Hydra?

Answer: Reproduction in Hydra is carried out through the process of budding, where a small bulb-like projection or bud grows on the parent body, develops into a small individual, and finally detaches to grow independently.

3. How is fertilisation in a hen different from fertilisation in a frog?

Answer: The difference is because a hen undergoes internal fertilisation where the fusion of gametes occurs inside the female body, whereas a frog undergoes external fertilisation where the egg and sperm fuse outside the body in water.

4. State the difference between oviparous and viviparous animals.

Answer: Oviparous animals are those that lay eggs outside their body to hatch young ones, whereas viviparous animals give birth directly to fully developed young ones.

5. Why is the number of eggs, laid by frogs at a time, much higher than those laid by a hen?

Answer: The number of eggs laid by frogs is much higher because frogs lay eggs in water where they are exposed to harsh weather conditions and predators, so laying thousands of eggs ensures that at least a few survive to become adult frogs.

6. How will you differentiate between a zygote and an embryo?

Answer: A zygote is a single-cell stage formed immediately after the fusion of sperm and egg, whereas an embryo is a multicellular stage formed by the repeated division of the zygote.

7. It is the mother who gives birth to a child. How do children, then, get features of both the parents?

Answer: Children get features of both parents because a child is formed by the fusion of a male gamete from the father and a female gamete from the mother, meaning both parents contribute equal genetic material to the child.

E. Answer the Following Questions

1. Define the term 'Metamorphosis'. List the changes that a tadpole undergoes to develop into a frog.

Answer: The process of transformation of a larva into an adult through drastic morphological changes during its life cycle is called metamorphosis. A tadpole develops long hind legs, loses its tail, develops lungs for breathing, and transforms its mouthparts to become an adult frog.

2. Trace the sequence of events that lead to development of a chick from the fertilised egg of a hen.

Answer: After internal fertilisation, the zygote divides repeatedly while traveling down the oviduct, forming protective shell layers around it. Once the hen lays the egg, she sits on it to provide warmth (incubation), and inside, the embryo develops fully into a chick over 3 weeks, which finally hatches by breaking the shell.

3. Where does fertilisation occur in human beings? How does the zygote formed eventually develop into an infant?

Answer: Fertilisation in humans occurs inside the oviduct (fallopian tube). The resulting zygote divides repeatedly to form a ball of cells called an embryo, which travels down to embed itself in the uterine wall (implantation); there, it develops body parts over 9 months as a foetus until birth.

6. Write the function(s) of each of the following parts of the human female reproductive system:

(a) Ovaries

Ovaries produce the female gametes called ova or eggs and secrete female sex hormones.

(b) Oviduct

Oviduct serves as the site for the fertilisation of the egg by a sperm and carries the zygote toward the uterus.

(c) Uterus

Uterus provides the site for the implantation, nourishment, and development of the embryo into a baby.
