D. Answer the Following Questions in Brief

1. State the appropriate term, used for a liquid or solution, which can conduct electricity (along with some accompanying chemical changes).

Answer: The appropriate term is electrolyte.

2. Distinguish between strong and weak electrolytes. Give two examples of each.

Answer: Strong electrolytes dissociate completely into ions and conduct electricity very well. Examples are Hydrochloric acid and Copper sulphate solution. Weak electrolytes dissociate only partially into ions and conduct electricity poorly. Examples are Acetic acid and Tap water.

3. Name the device that converts—
(a) Chemical energy into electrical energy.

(a) The device is an electric cell (or battery).

(b) Mechanical energy into electrical energy.

(b) The device is an electric generator.

4. Name the scientists who introduced the (scientific) world to the—
(a) voltaic cell

(a) The scientist is Alessandro Volta.

(b) phenomenon of electrolysis

(b) The scientist is Michael Faraday.

(c) phenomenon of electro-magnetic induction

(c) The scientist is Michael Faraday.

5. An Electrode 'A' is connected to the positive terminal while Electrode 'B' is connected to the negative terminal of a battery, as shown in the diagram.

(a) Give the names of the Electrodes A and B.

(a) Electrode A is Anode and Electrode B is Cathode.

(b) Name the process associated with the circuit shown here.

(b) The process is electrolysis of water.

(c) Name the gases produced at A and B.

(c) Gas produced at A (Anode) is Oxygen and at B (Cathode) is Hydrogen.

6. State the meaning of the terms:
(a) Electro-magnetic induction

(a) Electro-magnetic induction is the phenomenon of producing an electric current in a closed circuit due to a change in the magnetic field linked with it.

(b) Electrolysis

(b) Electrolysis is the chemical decomposition of an electrolyte by passing an electric current through it.

(c) Electrodes

(c) Electrodes are the metallic rods or plates dipped in an electrolyte through which electric current enters or leaves the solution.

(d) Electrorefining of metals

(d) Electrorefining is the process of purifying impure metals using the method of electrolysis.

7. State three uses of the phenomenon of electrolysis.

Answer: Three uses of electrolysis are extraction of metals from their ores, electrorefining of impure metals, and electroplating of base metals.

E. Answer the Following Questions

1. Give reasons for the following:
(a) Solid sodium chloride does not conduct electricity while sodium chloride solution conducts.

(a) Solid sodium chloride does not conduct electricity because its ions are held tightly in a fixed grid and cannot move, whereas its solution conducts because the water allows the ions to move freely and carry the current.

(b) It is not safe to carry out electrical repairs outdoors during a heavy downpour.

(b) It is not safe because rainwater contains dissolved impurities, making it a good conductor of electricity, which can cause severe electric shocks during repairs.

(c) To carry out electrolysis of water, a few drops of sulphuric acid are (carefully) added to the water.

(c) A few drops of sulphuric acid are added because pure water is a very poor conductor of electricity, and adding acid increases its conductivity.

(d) Kitchen gas burners are often coated with chromium.

(d) Kitchen gas burners are often coated with chromium because chromium is corrosion-resistant, scratch-resistant, and gives a shiny appearance.

2. Three electrolytic cells A, B and C are connected with identical bulbs in separate circuits as shown in the diagram. Electrolytic cell A contains sodium chloride solution, cell B contains acetic acid solution, and cell C contains distilled water. The solutions are (carefully) added to the water.

(a) In which Set-up will the bulb glow the brightest?

(a) The bulb will glow the brightest in Set-up A containing sodium chloride solution.

(b) In which Set-up will the glow of the bulb be quite dim?

(b) The bulb will be quite dim in Set-up B containing acetic acid solution.

(c) In which Set-up will the bulb not glow at all? Justify your answers.

(c) The bulb will not glow at all in Set-up C containing distilled water. This is because sodium chloride is a strong electrolyte, acetic acid is a weak electrolyte, and distilled water has very few free ions to conduct electricity.

4. (a) Define electroplating. How can we coat spoons with silver? Explain with the help of a diagram.

(a) Electroplating is the process of depositing a thin layer of a superior metal over a base metal object using an electric current. To coat a spoon with silver, the spoon is made the cathode (negative electrode), a pure plate of silver is made the anode (positive electrode), and they are dipped in a silver nitrate solution. Passing current deposits pure silver on the spoon.

(b) State any three uses of electroplating.

(b) Three uses are coating iron ornaments with gold or silver for appearance, coating car parts with chromium to prevent rust, and coating iron cans with tin for food storage.

5. Describe an experiment to show that we can get electrical energy by using a magnet.

Answer: Take a long insulated copper wire and wind it into a cylindrical coil. Connect the ends of the coil to a sensitive galvanometer or a torch bulb. When a strong bar magnet is pushed rapidly inside the coil, the bulb glows momentarily, showing that relative motion between a magnet and a coil produces electrical energy.

6. A coil of insulated copper wire is connected to a torch bulb. State the likely 'response' of the bulb, if a bar magnet is—

(a) pushed into the coil?

(a) The bulb will glow momentarily.

(b) pulled out of the coil?

(b) The bulb will glow momentarily again, but the induced current flows in the opposite direction.

(c) held stationary within the coil?

(c) The bulb will not glow at all because there is no relative motion to change the magnetic field.
