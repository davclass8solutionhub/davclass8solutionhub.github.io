4. Define the following:
(a) Milky Way

(a) The Milky Way is the home galaxy of our solar system, consisting of billions of stars, dust and gas bound together by gravity.

(b) Celestial objects

(b) Celestial objects are natural objects existing in outer space, such as stars, planets, the Moon and asteroids.

(c) Pole Star

(c) The Pole Star is a star that appears almost stationary in the northern sky and helps in locating the north direction.

(d) Constellation

(d) A constellation is a group of stars forming a recognizable pattern or shape in the night sky.

(e) Asteroids

(e) Asteroids are small rocky celestial bodies that revolve around the Sun, mostly between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter.

(f) Geo-stationary Satellites

(f) Geo-stationary satellites are artificial satellites that revolve around the Earth at the same rate as the Earth's rotation and appear stationary over a fixed point.

(g) Comets

(g) Comets are small celestial bodies made mainly of ice and dust that revolve around the Sun in highly elliptical orbits and develop a tail near the Sun.

(h) Light year

(h) A light year is the astronomical unit of distance equal to the distance travelled by light in one year.

(i) Meteors

(i) Meteors are small rocky or metallic bodies that enter the Earth's atmosphere at high speed and produce a bright streak of light when they burn due to friction.

(j) Meteorites

(j) Meteorites are pieces of meteors that survive their passage through the atmosphere and reach the Earth's surface.

5. Name the visible layer of the sun. Give an estimate of its temperature.

Answer: The visible layer of the Sun is called the photosphere. Its estimated surface temperature is about 6000 °C.

2. Differentiate between the following, giving examples:
(a) stars and constellations

(a) A star is a single huge ball of glowing gas that produces its own light, such as the Sun. A constellation is a group of stars forming a recognizable pattern, such as Ursa Major.

(b) stars and planets

(b) Stars produce their own light and heat, such as the Sun. Planets do not produce their own light and revolve around a star, such as the Earth.

(c) terrestrial planets and jovian planets

(c) Terrestrial planets are the four inner rocky planets, such as Mars. Jovian planets are the four outer large planets, such as Jupiter.

(d) artificial and natural satellite

(d) An artificial satellite is a man-made object launched into space, such as INSAT. A natural satellite is a naturally occurring body revolving around a planet, such as the Moon.

4. Give reasons for the following:
(a) Uranus is observed to have a blue-green colour.

(a) Uranus is observed to have a blue-green colour because methane gas in its atmosphere absorbs much of the red light and allows blue-green light to be reflected.

(b) Venus is the hottest planet even though Mercury is closest to the Sun.

(b) Venus is the hottest planet because its thick atmosphere contains a large amount of carbon dioxide, which causes a strong greenhouse effect and traps heat.

(c) The shape and size of bright side of moon appears to change (gradually) everyday.

(c) The shape and size of the bright side of the Moon appears to change because the Moon revolves around the Earth, changing the relative positions of the Sun, Moon and Earth.

(d) A star appears to rise four minutes earlier in the sky everyday than the previous day.

(d) A star appears to rise about four minutes earlier each day because the Earth revolves around the Sun while rotating on its axis, so it has to rotate slightly more each day to face the same star.

(e) Comets become visible only when they are near the Sun.

(e) Comets become visible near the Sun because the Sun's heat causes their frozen material to vaporise and form a bright coma and tail that can be seen from Earth.

5. State, in brief, how artificial satellites are used in transmitting television programmes over large distances.

Answer: Artificial satellites receive television signals from a ground station, amplify them and retransmit them back to Earth over a large area, allowing television programmes to be transmitted over long distances.
