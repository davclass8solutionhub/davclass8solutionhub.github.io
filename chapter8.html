D. Answer the Following

1. How have urbanisation and industrialisation led to a large scale deforestation?
Answer:
Urbanisation and industrialisation have led to large scale deforestation because forest land is being sacrificed for massive development projects. Forests are also used as a source of raw material for industries like paper, pulp, and timber.
2. A new species X is introduced in a forest. How is it likely to affect the local species of the area?
Answer:
The introduced species is likely to affect the local species because it may prey on, or compete with, the native local species for resources, causing ecosystem disruption.
3. What is a Biosphere Reserve? Name two biosphere reserves in India.
Answer:
A biosphere reserve is an international conservation designation given by UNESCO. Two examples of biosphere reserves in India are Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve and Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve.
4. How does the Red Data Book help in protecting biodiversity?
Answer:
The Red Data Book helps in protecting biodiversity by providing useful information on the threat status of different species and maintaining a list of species whose existence is threatened.
5. Why do some birds and animals migrate?
Answer:
Some birds and animals migrate because they move from their original home to other places to find abundant food and a favourable place to breed.
6. How do birds get helped in navigation while undertaking long distance migration?
Answer:
Birds get helped in navigation while undertaking long distance migration by using the position of the sun and stars, visual landmarks, and an inbuilt magnetic compass in their body.
7. Distinguish between a National Park and a Wildlife Sanctuary.
Answer:
National Park Wildlife Sanctuary
It is a reserve of land protected from most human development works and pollution. It is an officially designated space where hunting, poaching, or capturing of animals is strictly prohibited.

E. Answer the Following

1. Explain how deforestation can result in droughts and floods.
Answer:
Deforestation can result in droughts and floods in the following ways:
  • Droughts: Fewer trees mean more carbon dioxide in the atmosphere, leading to global warming. This disturbs the water cycle, causing fewer rains and droughts.
  • Floods: Deforestation reduces the soil's capacity to absorb water. There is a lack of trees to bind the soil, leading to increased instances of flooding.
2. 'Extinction of tiger can destabilise the entire forest ecosystem and result in large scale loss of biodiversity.' Justify this statement.
Answer:
This statement is justified because conserving biodiversity is important as each group of organisms has its own important role to play in an ecosystem. For example, if the tiger in a forest is killed, the population of deer will increase uncontrollably. This will deplete the vegetation, which will eventually destroy the habitat and destabilise the entire ecosystem.
3. Why are endemic species at a greater risk of becoming extinct? List the human action related causes that are responsible for their extinction.
Answer:
Endemic species are at a greater risk of becoming extinct because they are found only in a particular area, so any change in their habitat easily threatens their existence. The human action related causes responsible for their extinction are:
  • Massive development projects and urbanisation.
  • Destruction and fragmentation of their natural habitat.
  • Introduction of new alien species that compete with them.
4. 'The growing human population poses a grave threat to biodiversity.' Justify this statement.
Answer:
This statement is justified because a significant percentage of the growing human population depends on forests for timber, firewood, fodder, and building materials. To meet these daily requirements, there is an indiscriminate cutting down of trees, known as deforestation. This destroys the natural habitat of plants and animals, posing a grave threat to biodiversity.
5. Explain the difference between extinct, endangered and vulnerable species.
Answer:
The difference between extinct, endangered and vulnerable species can be explained as follows:
  • Extinct Species: These are species in which no member exists, or the species is presumed beyond reasonable doubt to have disappeared. Example: Dodo.
  • Endangered Species: These are species that face a very high risk of extinction in the near future. Examples: Blue whale and Giant panda.
  • Vulnerable Species: These are species that face a high risk of extinction in the medium term and will become endangered soon if threats continue. Examples: Cheetah and Gaur.
6. List five ways in which you, as an individual, can contribute to conserving forests and wildlife.
Answer:
As an individual, I can contribute to conserving forests and wildlife in the following five ways:
  • Reduce the wastage of paper and try to reuse paper whenever possible.
  • Participate in reforestation by planting trees in areas where none existed before.
  • Follow the principles of 'Reduce, Re-use, Recycle'.
  • Spread awareness about the protection of local flora and fauna.
  • Strictly avoid purchasing products made from endangered wildlife parts.
