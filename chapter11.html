D. Answer the Following Questions in Brief

1. Name the protective part of the human eye.

Answer: Cornea.

2. Why does the pupil appear black?

Answer: The pupil appears black because no light is reflected back and the entering light is absorbed inside the eye.

3. Differentiate between rod-shaped cells and cone-shaped cells.

(a) Rod-shaped cells

Rod-shaped cells are sensitive to dim light.

(b) Cone-shaped cells

Cone-shaped cells respond to bright light and help us see colours.

4. Differentiate between near point and far point.

(a) Near point

The near point is the closest distance at which a normal eye can see an object clearly. It is about 25 cm.

(b) Far point

The far point is the maximum distance at which a normal eye can see clearly. It is infinity.

5. Define the following:

(a) Blind spot

A blind spot is the point where the optic nerve leaves the eyeball. It has no sensory cells, so no image is formed there.

(b) Persistence of vision

Persistence of vision is the ability of the eye to retain the impression of an image on the retina for about 1/16th of a second.

6. What is meant by the following?

(a) Power of accommodation

The power of accommodation is the ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length to see near and distant objects clearly.

(b) Least distance of distinct vision

The least distance of distinct vision is the minimum distance at which a normal eye can see an object clearly without strain. It is about 25 cm.

(c) Range of vision

The range of vision is the distance between the near point and the far point of the eye. For a normal eye, it is from 25 cm to infinity.

7. As the distance of an object from the eye increases, why does the image distance in the eye remain constant?

Answer: The ciliary muscles adjust the focal length of the eye lens so that the image remains formed on the retina.

8. Sahil cannot see a text clearly when it is kept at 25 cm from his eyes.

(a) Where is the image formed in his eye?

The image is formed beyond the retina.

(b) What does this indicate about his least distance of distinct vision?

His least distance of distinct vision is greater than 25 cm, indicating hypermetropia.

9. A myopic person cannot see objects clearly beyond 1.2 m. What is the likely cause and how can it be corrected?

Answer: The likely cause is that the eyeball is larger than normal. It can be corrected using a concave lens.

E. Answer the Following Questions

1. Draw a labelled diagram of the human eye and explain the functions of its main parts.

(a) Cornea

The cornea protects the inner parts of the eye and allows light to enter.

(b) Iris

The iris controls the amount of light entering the eye through the pupil.

(c) Pupil

The pupil allows light to enter the eye.

(d) Eye lens

The eye lens focuses light to form an image on the retina.

(e) Ciliary muscles

The ciliary muscles adjust the focal length of the eye lens.

(f) Retina

The retina is a light-sensitive screen where the image is formed.

(g) Optic nerve

The optic nerve carries signals from the eye to the brain.

Diagram is given on page 200.

2. Explain how we perceive objects as they are.

Answer: Light rays from an object form an image on the retina. The optic nerve sends signals to the brain, and the brain interprets them.

3. List five precautions for taking care of the eyes.

Answer:

  • Wash the eyes with clean water.
  • Do not read in dim or very bright light.
  • Maintain a proper reading distance.
  • Avoid reading while travelling in a moving vehicle.
  • Do not rub the eyes if dust enters them.

4. Explain how optical and non-optical aids help visually challenged persons.

Answer: Optical aids help improve vision, while non-optical aids such as Braille and audiobooks help visually challenged persons read, learn and work independently.

5. What is the Braille system? How does it help visually challenged persons?

Answer: Braille is a system of raised dots used for reading and writing. It helps visually challenged persons read, write and study using their fingertips.
