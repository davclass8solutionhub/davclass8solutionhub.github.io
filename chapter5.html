Chapter 5: Friction Solutions

A. Fill in the blanks

1. Friction always ________ the relative motion between the two surfaces that are in contact with each other.
Answer: opposes
2. ________ friction is always greater than sliding friction.
Answer: Static
3. Sportsmen use shoes fitted with ________ to increase friction between their shoes and the ground.
Answer: spikes
4. Sprinkling ________ powder on the carrom board helps to decrease friction.
Answer: talcum
5. Frictional force on an object moving in a fluid depends on its nature, speed and the ________ of the object.
Answer: shape
6. The shape of an aeroplane is ________ to reduce effects of friction due to air.
Answer: streamlined

Answer: False

Answer: True
4. Soles of shoes and tyres are treaded to decrease effects of friction.
Answer: False
5. Lubricants can be liquids, semi-liquids or solids.
Answer: True

C. Answer the following

1. A rubber ball is placed on a concrete floor, carpet and glass sheet. Arrange these surfaces in increasing order of friction.
Answer: Glass sheet < Concrete floor < Carpet.
2. Arrange static friction, sliding friction and rolling friction in increasing order.
Answer: Rolling friction < Sliding friction < Static friction.
3. When does friction increase?
Answer: Friction increases when two surfaces are pressed harder against each other.
4. On which surface will a toy car experience minimum friction?
Answer: A wet marble floor.
5. Why are lubricants used?
Answer: Lubricants smoothen the surfaces and reduce friction.
6. Does fluid friction depend upon the colour of the fluid?
Answer: No. Fluid friction does not depend upon the colour of the fluid.

D. Answer the following questions

1. Define the terms: (a) static friction (b) sliding friction.
(a) Static friction
Answer: Static friction is the friction between two surfaces when there is no relative motion between them. It is self-adjusting and increases with the applied force up to a certain limit.
(b) Sliding friction
Answer: Sliding friction is the friction that acts when one body slides over another. It is also called kinetic friction and is slightly less than static friction.
2. Differentiate between rolling friction and static friction.
Rolling Friction Static Friction
Rolling friction acts when an object rolls over another surface. Static friction acts between two surfaces when there is no relative motion between them.
Rolling friction is much less than sliding friction. Static friction increases with the applied force up to a limiting value.
Example: A ball rolling on the ground. Example: A box resting on the floor.
3. State the likely causes of friction.
Answer: The likely causes of friction are the interlocking of irregularities on the surfaces in contact and the force pressing the two surfaces together.
4. A box is resting on the floor. Different forces are applied to it as shown in the given figures.
(a) Give the likely value of limiting friction if the applied force is between 2F and 3F.
Answer: The limiting friction is likely to be more than 2F and less than 3F.
(b) Why does the box remain at rest in the given cases?
Answer: The box remains at rest because the applied force is less than the limiting friction.
(c) In which direction does friction act in each case? State whether the friction is static or sliding.
Answer: Friction acts in the direction opposite to the applied force or the tendency of motion. In cases (a) and (b), the friction is static, while in case (c), the friction is sliding.
5. A book is placed on cylindrical pencils and moved by pulling it. What type of friction acts here?
Answer: Rolling friction acts here.
6. Why are objects given special shapes when they move through fluids? What are these shapes called?
Answer: Objects are given special shapes to minimise friction due to fluids. These shapes are called streamlined shapes.

E. Give answers to the following

1. Define friction. State the factors affecting friction and give an example.
Answer: Friction is the force that opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact. It depends mainly on the nature of the surfaces and the force pressing them together. For example, when a ball rolls towards the east, friction acts towards the west and eventually stops the ball.
2. A ball rolls down an inclined surface and then moves on horizontal surfaces A, B and C. On which surface will the ball travel the longest distance? Give reason.
Answer: The ball will travel the longest distance on surface C, the glass sheet. A smooth surface has less interlocking between its irregularities and therefore offers less friction.
3. Why is sliding friction slightly less than static friction?
Answer: In static friction, the irregularities of the two surfaces get strongly interlocked. During sliding, the interlocking is weaker, so sliding friction is slightly less than static friction.
4. Give reasons for the following:
(a) Why do we slip on a banana peel?
Answer: A banana peel provides very little friction, so we may slip on it.
(b) Why is it easier to move a lighter box than a heavier box?
Answer: Friction depends on the force pressing the surfaces together. A heavier box experiences greater friction.
(c) Why is more force needed to start a car than to keep it moving at a uniform speed?
Answer: Static friction is greater than sliding friction, so more force is needed to start the car.
(d) Why do sportsmen use shoes fitted with spikes?
Answer: Spikes increase friction and provide better grip with the ground.
(e) Why are ball bearings used in machines?
Answer: Ball bearings reduce friction by replacing sliding friction with rolling friction.
(f) Why are tyres changed regularly?
Answer: Tyres wear out due to friction. Worn tyres provide less grip and may cause slipping.
(g) Why are machine parts oiled or greased?
Answer: Oil and grease reduce friction between moving parts and increase the efficiency of machines.
(h) Why are sports cars, aeroplanes and boats given streamlined shapes?
Answer: Streamlined shapes minimise fluid friction and allow these objects to move more easily through air or water.
(i) Why are chains fitted around tyres when vehicles move on icy roads?
Answer: Chains increase friction and provide better grip on icy roads.
(j) Why are rollers used for carrying heavy luggage?
Answer: Rollers make movement easier because rolling friction is much less than sliding friction.
5. How can friction help us in motion? Give three examples.
Answer:
  • While walking, we push the ground backward and friction provides a forward reaction.
  • Vehicles can move because of friction between their tyres and the road.
  • We can write because friction provides grip between a pen or pencil and paper.
