Section D: Answer the Following Questions in Brief

1. Define the term 'pollutant'.

Answer: Any unwanted substance that contaminates the environment and causes harmful effects on living and non-living things is called a pollutant.

2. State two causes of air pollution.

Answer: Two major causes of air pollution are the burning of fossil fuels in vehicles and the release of harmful gases from factories.

3. How is carboxy haemoglobin formed?

Carboxy haemoglobin is formed because carbon monoxide gas released from incomplete combustion binds very strongly with the haemoglobin present in our blood cells.

4. State the harmful effects of chlorofluorocarbons.

The harmful effect of chlorofluorocarbons is that they decompose in the upper atmosphere to deplete the ozone layer, which allows dangerous ultraviolet rays to reach the earth.

5. Suggest any three measures that can help to check global warming.

Three measures to check global warming are planting more trees, reducing the use of fossil fuels, and adopting clean alternative energy sources like solar and wind power.

Section E: Answer the Following Questions

1. Give the meaning of the term 'smog'. List its harmful effects.

Smog is a thick dark mixture of smoke and fog that forms in the atmosphere during cold days. Its harmful effects include causing severe breathing difficulties like asthma, bronchitis, and continuous coughing in human beings.

2. Write a short note on 'acid rain'.

Acid rain is formed because polluting gases like sulphur dioxide and nitrogen dioxide react with water vapour in clouds to produce sulphuric and nitric acids, which then fall down with rain. It damages soil, water bodies, and historic monuments.

3. List the main causes of increase in concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere.

The main causes are the excessive burning of fossil fuels in industries and vehicles, rapid deforestation that reduces carbon dioxide absorption, and agricultural activities that release methane gas.

4. State the likely consequences of an increase in the 'greenhouse effect'.

An increase in the greenhouse effect leads to global warming, which causes the rapid melting of polar glaciers, rising sea levels that flood coastal regions, and unexpected shifts in worldwide weather patterns.

5. Write any five methods that can help to control air pollution.

Five methods to control air pollution are: using cleaner fuels like CNG in vehicles, fitting tall chimneys with electrostatic precipitators in factories, promoting large-scale plantation of trees, avoiding the open burning of dry leaves or garbage, and switching to renewable energy sources like solar power.
