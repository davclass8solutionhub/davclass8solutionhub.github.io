D. Answer the following questions in brief.

1. Why are viruses considered as being at the 'borderline' between living and non-living things?
Ans: Viruses are considered being at the 'borderline' between living and non-living things because when they come in contact with a suitable plant, animal or bacteria they show the characteristics of living organisms while and when they are floating in air or are settled on a door knob, they are non-living like say salt and sugar regarded as being borderline between living and non-living entities.
2. What is fermentation? How is this process useful in food and beverage industries?
Ans: Fermentation is anaerobic process through which sugar/carbohydrates are converted into alcohol or acids and carbon dioxide by microorganisms is called fermentation. Fermentation is useful in food and beverage industries it helps in making idly dosa, it helps in making bread and cake.
3. In the absence of microorganisms the earth would become a heap of dead plants and animals. Justify this statement.
Ans: When a plant or animal dies, it leaves behind nutrients and energy inside organic matter that fixed its body structure and it is decomposed by microorganisms and convert all such organic matter into carbon-dioxide and nutrients. These nutrients become a part of soil and this process replenishes nutrients back to the ecosystem. By microorganisms this process occur and help to cleaning the environment. So, in the absence of microorganisms the earth would become a heap of dead plants and animals.
4. Munna observes that the yield of wheat growing in his field has reduced in the current year. He gets the soil tested and the report confirms the deficiency of one particular nutrient. Accordingly he is advised to grow peas after harvesting wheat.

(i) Name the nutrient found insufficient.
(ii) How do you think growing peas will help in replenishing the soil?
(i) Ans: Nitrogen

(ii) Ans: Pea plants possess nodules in Rhizobium bacteria. These bacteria take nitrogen from atmosphere and fix into soil in replenishing soil.
5. Why does it take less time to prepare curd in summers as compared to preparing it in winters?
Ans: Microorganism reproduce at a faster rate between 30°C to 40°C. They reproduce at a much slower rate when temperature is below or this range. Milk is converted into curd by multiplication of Lactobacillus. So, it takes less time to prepare curd in summers as compared to preparing it in winters.
6. What are antibiotics? How are they produced? Give two examples of these.
Ans: Antibiotics is chemical produce by microorganism that kill or inhibit the growth of other harmful microorganisms by affecting their life processes. Two ex - penicillin and streptomycin.
7. How are the following diseases transmitted:
(a) malaria (b) common cold (c) tetanus (d) typhoid
Ans:
(a) malaria -> By female anopheles mosquito
(b) common cold -> Through cough droplets in air
(c) tetanus -> Through cut by a rusted iron articles
(d) typhoid -> Through contaminated water.

E. Answer the following questions.

1. 'Microorganisms are very useful in manufacturing different food items.' Explain the above statement.
Ans: Microorganisms are very useful in manufacturing different food items in many ways like:
They are used in making idly dosa.
They are used in making bread and cake.
They are also used in manufacture of alcohol, wine.
So, Microorganisms are very useful in manufacturing different food items.
2. Give reasons for the following:
(a) Yeast is added to the batter used to make 'dhokla'.
(b) It is important to brush one's teeth before going to bed.
(c) Reducing the quantity of salt in pickles can cause it to go bad soon.
(d) Foods from puffed or enlarged cans should not be consumed.
(e) When using frozen food items one should take out only the requirement quantities; thawed food should never be refrozen.
(a) Ans: Yeast is added to the batter used to make 'dhokla' that fermentation take place. It can cause to increase in volume and makes it fluffy and soft.

(b) Ans: It is important to brush one's teeth before going to bed because bacteria can grow on food particles that lodged in between teeth. Acid produced by those bacteria corrode the tooth enamel and cause cavities. So, it is important to brush one's teeth before going to bed.

(c) Ans: Salt reduce the water content of pickle and make it unavailable for the growth of microorganism. So, pickle is preserved. If the quantity of salt is reduced then it increases the growth of microorganisms and can it to go bad soon.

(d) Ans: Foods from puffed or enlarged cans should not be consumed because they shows that there had been some bacterial action on the item which might lead to formation of toxic substance.

(e) Ans: One should take out only required quantity of frozen food as it might spoil rest of food item and refrozen destroys its nutrients.
3. How do communicable diseases spread? Suggest ways to prevent the following diseases:
(a) Tuberculosis (b) Athlete's foot (c) cholera
Ans: Communicable diseases spread from one person to another through air, water, food, physical contact or insects.

The ways to prevent the following diseases are:
(a) Tuberculosis -> Tuberculosis is prevented by vaccination.
(b) Athlete's foot -> Athlete's foot is prevented by maintaining good personal hygiene.
(c) cholera -> cholera is prevented by proper disposal of waste and using disinfected water.
4. What are pathogens? Name two pathogen each that cause disease in (i) plants and (ii) animal.
Ans: Some microorganisms are harmful in different ways. They cause disease in human beings, plant and animal. Such disease causing organism are known as pathogens.
Ex in plants -> Bacteria and fungi
Ex in animals -> Pathogens
5. Mohan bought samosas from a road-side vendor and ate it. After sometime he felt nausea and started vomiting. He had severe pain in the abdominal region and suffered from diarrhoea. What could be the reason for his condition? What is it called? How could it be prevented?
Ans: The reason for his condition is consumption of contaminated food. This disease is called food poisoning. Food must be properly prepared and store to prevent food poisoning.
6. Which diseases the following children are most likely to suffer from?
(a) Ramu drinks water from a nearby lake. This water is neither boiled nor disinfected.
Ans: Cholera
(b) Ashu does not take a bath everyday. He maintains poor personal hygiene.
Ans: Ringworm
(c) Pulkit has been bitten by an infected street dog.
Ans: Rabies
(d) Sohan is living in an area where the population is very high. He does not use a mosquito net (while sleeping), or insect-repellent creams (while playing in the open).
Ans: Malaria
(e) Mohan was with two of his friends who were coughing and sneezing (they were suffering from common cold). One of these friends did not keep a handkerchief in front of his mouth while coughing and sneezing.
Ans: Common Cold
