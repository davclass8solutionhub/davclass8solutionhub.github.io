D. Answer the Following

1. Why is the use of a seed drill more advantageous as compared to sowing seeds by broadcasting?
Answer:
The use of a seed drill is more advantageous as compared to sowing seeds by broadcasting because it sows the seeds at correct depths and proper distances, ensures uniform availability of sunlight, water and nutrients, and saves time and labour.
2. Babu wants to practice crop rotation. Help him by suggesting what kind of crops can be grown between two consecutive cereal crops? Also, provide him names of one Rabi and one Kharif crop that can be used to replenish the nitrogen content of the soil.
Answer:
Leguminous crops can be grown between two consecutive cereal crops. One Rabi crop that can be used to replenish the nitrogen content of the soil is pea or bean, and one Kharif crop is soyabean.
3. If rice is sown in the month of November, how is the produce likely to be impacted? And why?
Answer:
If rice is sown in the month of November, the produce will be badly impacted because rice is a Kharif crop that requires lots of water and stands in standing water, which is not available in November during the winter season.
4. What are crumbs? Why should they be broken?
Answer:
Crumbs are big pieces of soil in the ploughed field. They should be broken because breaking them helps in proper levelling of the field, prevents soil erosion by wind or water, and ensures uniform irrigation.
5. Define the term 'transplantation'. List any two advantages it offers to the farmer.
Answer:
Transplantation is the process of transferring healthy seedlings from nursery beds and planting them in the main field. It offers the following advantages:
  • It enables the farmer to selectively cultivate only healthy plants.
  • It ensures better root penetration and development of shoot.

E. Answer the Following

1. State the advantages of ploughing the soil. State the different ways through which it is done.
Answer:
The advantages of ploughing the soil are:
  • It loosens the soil, allowing roots to breathe easily and penetrate deeper.
  • It enhances the water-retaining capacity of the soil.
  • It helps in uprooting weeds and mixing nutrients uniformly.
The different ways through which ploughing is done are manually using a wooden or iron plough drawn by oxen, or mechanically using a tractor-driven cultivator.
2. Both manures and fertilisers replenish soil fertility. Compare and contrast the advantages and disadvantages of using them.
Answer:
Type Advantages Disadvantages
Manures Safe, natural, enhance water-holding capacity, and improve soil texture. Not nutrient-specific, bulky, and slow to show results.
Fertilisers Rich in specific nutrients (N, P, K), readily absorbed, and easy to store. Overuse changes chemical nature of soil, reduces long-term fertility, and causes pollution.
3. Explain how weeds are harmful for plants. State the different ways of weed removal practiced by farmers.
Answer:
Weeds are harmful for plants because they compete with crop plants for water, nutrients, space, and light, thereby reducing crop growth. The different ways of weed removal practiced by farmers are:
  • Manual removal: Weeds are uprooted or cut close to the ground using a khurpi or sickle.
  • Tilling: The field is ploughed before sowing to uproot and kill weeds.
  • Weedicides: Chemicals like 2,4-D are sprayed to destroy weeds without damaging crops.
4. Give reasons for the following:
Answer:
(a) Excess water supply can damage crops because it leads to water-logging, which cuts off atmospheric air from the roots and causes them to rot.

(b) Grains are not stored without sun-drying because fresh grains contain moisture, which promotes the growth of microbes, fungi, and pests that spoil the crop.

(c) Overuse of pesticides should be avoided because it changes the chemical nature of the soil, kills useful organisms, causes environmental pollution, and enters the food chain.
5. Bhola's field is ready with a mature wheat crop. List and explain the steps he would have to undertake before selling the grains to the FCI (Food Corporation of India).
Answer:
The steps Bhola would have to undertake before selling the grains to the FCI are:
  • Harvesting: The mature wheat crop is cut and gathered using a sickle or combine harvester.
  • Threshing: The grains are separated from the harvested crop.
  • Winnowing: The clean wheat grains are separated from the chaff.
  • Sun-drying: The separated grains are dried under the sun to reduce moisture before storage.
  • Storage and Packing: The dried grains are packed properly in gunny bags to protect them from pests before transporting them to the FCI.
6. Define hybridisation. List the steps to be followed for obtaining seeds of a hybrid variety.
Answer:
Hybridisation is a method of developing new, high-yielding crop varieties by cross-breeding two different varieties of plants. The steps to be followed for obtaining seeds of a hybrid variety are:
  • Selection: Two plant varieties (A and B) with desired characteristics are selected.
  • Emasculation: The anthers are removed from the flower of plant A to prevent self-pollination.
  • Pollination: Pollen grains from plant B are dusted onto the stigma of plant A.
  • Bagging: The cross-pollinated flower is covered with a paper bag to prevent unwanted pollination until seeds develop.
