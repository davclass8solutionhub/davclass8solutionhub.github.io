D. Answer the Following Questions in Brief

1. Name the cause that is regarded as the basic cause of all sounds.

Answer: The basic cause of all sounds is vibrations in a body.

2. Name two musical instruments which produce sound through vibrating strings.

Answer: Two musical instruments are guitar and sitar.

3. Through which state of matter does sound propagate with the—

(a) slowest speed?

Sound propagates with the slowest speed through gases.

(b) fastest speed?

Sound propagates with the fastest speed through solids.

4. Define the following terms. Also, write their SI units:

(a) amplitude

The maximum displacement of a vibrating body from its central position is called amplitude. Its SI unit is meter (m).

(b) time period

The time taken by a vibrating body to complete one full vibration is called time period. Its SI unit is second (s).

(c) frequency

The number of vibrations produced by a body in one second is called frequency. Its SI unit is hertz (Hz).

5. Name the characteristic of a vibrating body which determines the—

(a) loudness

Loudness is determined by the amplitude of vibrations.

(b) pitch of the sound produced by it.

Pitch is determined by the frequency of vibrations.

6. The frequencies of the male and female voices in human beings are nearly 1 kHz and 1.5 kHz. Which of the two voices—

(a) is likely to be flatter?

The male voice (1 kHz) is likely to be flatter because its frequency is lower.

(b) has a higher pitch?

The female voice (1.5 kHz) has a higher pitch because its frequency is higher.

7. When is a given sound regarded as a 'musical sound' or 'music'?

Answer: A sound is regarded as a musical sound because it is produced by regular, periodic vibrations that are pleasing to the ears.

E. Answer the Following Questions

1. Explain briefly the mechanism of production of sound in human beings.

Answer: Sound in humans is produced by the voice box or larynx. When air from the lungs is forced through the narrow slit between the two vocal cords, it makes them vibrate, which creates the sound.

2. How does the mechanism of production of sounds in birds differ from that in human beings?

Answer: The mechanism differs because humans produce sound using a larynx with vocal cords at the top of the windpipe, whereas birds produce sound using a special structure called syrinx located at the lower end of their windpipe.

3. Describe a simple experiment to show that sound cannot propagate through vacuum.

Answer: Place an electric bell inside a sealed glass jar connected to a vacuum pump. When the bell rings, we can hear it clearly, but as the pump removes all the air to create a vacuum, the sound disappears completely even though the hammer is still seen striking the bell.

4. On a hot summer day, a little mosquito produces a warning sound near your ear by vibrating its wings at the rate of 3,000 wing beats per five seconds. State the frequency, in hertz, of this sound.

Answer: The frequency is 600 Hz because frequency equals total vibrations (3,000) divided by total time in seconds (5).

5. Explain why we always hear the thunder a little after we see the flash of lightning.

Answer: We always hear the thunder a little later because the speed of light (3,000,000,000 m/s) is vastly faster than the speed of sound in air (nearly 340 m/s), so light reaches us almost instantly while sound takes time.

6. (a) Define 'loudness' and 'pitch'.

Loudness is the characteristic that distinguishes a faint sound from a loud one, while pitch distinguishes a shrill sound from a grave or flat one.

(b) State the effect on the—

(i) loudness of a sound when the amplitude of the vibrating body increases.

The loudness increases and becomes louder when the amplitude increases.

(ii) pitch of a sound when the frequency of the vibrating body increases.

The pitch increases and the sound becomes sharper or more shrill when the frequency increases.

7. Why is noise pollution regarded as harmful for human beings?

Answer: Noise pollution is regarded as harmful because constant exposure to loud sounds can cause severe health issues like high blood pressure, lack of sleep, headache, stress, and even temporary or permanent hearing loss.
