D. Answer the following questions

1. What is a combustible material? Give two examples.
Answer:
A material which on heating in the presence of air/oxygen catches fire easily and produce heat and light energy is called combustible material. Ex- coal and wood.
2. What are the three conditions necessary for combustion?
Answer:
The three conditions needed for combustion are:
  • A combustible substance
  • Temperature should be more than ignition temperature
  • Oxygen or air
3. What is slow combustion?
Answer:
Slow combustion takes place in an insufficient supply of air.
4. What is meant by calorific value of a fuel?
Answer:
The amount of heat energy produced on completely burning one kilogram of a fuel in pure oxygen is called the calorific value of a fuel.
5. What are the characteristics of a good fuel?
Answer:
It should be cheap, readily available and easy to transport. It should have a high calorific value.
6. Why is water not used in controlling electrical fires?
Answer:
Water is not used in controlling electrical fires because it can cause electric shocks.

2. Calorific value of wood is 18,000 kJ. How much of wood is required to produce 3,60,000 kJ of heat energy?
Answer:
Calorific value of wood = 18,000 kJ/kg
Heat energy required = 3,60,000 kJ
Mass of wood required = Heat energy required ÷ Calorific value
= 3,60,000 ÷ 18,000
= 20 kg
Therefore, 20 kg of wood is required.
3. Why are gaseous fuels considered better than liquid or solid fuels?
Answer:
Gaseous fuels are considered better than liquid or solid fuels because gaseous fuels leave no ash or solid residue on combustion and their rate of combustion can be easily controlled with the help of a control valve, while solid and liquid fuels generally leave behind ash or residues on combustion and their rate of combustion cannot be easily controlled.
4. Why do we say that burning of fuel can cause health hazards?
Answer:
We say that burning of fuel can cause health hazards because fuels, like wood, coal and petroleum products, release unburnt carbon particles. These carbon particles in the air cause breathing problems. Incomplete combustion of carbon fuels results in the formation of carbon monoxide gas. It is a very poisonous gas. Even small amounts of it can cause breathing problems; large amounts of it can prove fatal.
