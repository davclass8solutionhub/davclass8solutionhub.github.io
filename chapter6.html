D. Answer the following questions in brief

1. Define the term 'fuel'.
Answer: The substances which produce heat and light energy on burning in air are called fuels.
2. State the conditions under which smouldering coal can be converted into synthetic petroleum.
Answer: Smouldering coal can be converted into synthetic petroleum by reacting it with hydrogen gas under high pressure and in the presence of suitable catalysts.
3. Define the term 'carbonisation'.
Answer: Carbonisation is the slow process of conversion of dead plant matter into coal under the high pressure and high temperature of the earth over a period of millions of years.
4. Name the different types of coal.
Answer: The different types of coal are peat, lignite, bituminous coal, and anthracite.
5. State the uses of (a) coke, and (b) coal gas.
(a) Coke
Answer: It is used as a domestic and industrial fuel, for extraction of metals, and to make fuel gases like water gas.
(b) Coal gas
Answer: It is used as an industrial fuel for those industries which are located near coal processing plants.
6. Name the principal products obtained by the refining of petroleum.
Answer: The principal products obtained by the refining of petroleum are LPG, petrol, kerosene, diesel, lubricating oil, paraffin wax, and bitumen.

E. Answer the following questions

1. Describe the process of 'formation of coal' in nature.
Answer: Coal was formed from plant remains buried deep under the earth's crust millions of years ago. Due to high temperature, high pressure, and the absence of air, these dead plant materials slowly converted into coal through carbonisation.
2. Differentiate between renewable and non-renewable sources of energy. Give examples of each.
Renewable Sources Non-renewable Sources
These are natural resources which get replenished through natural processes in a relatively short period of time. These are inexhaustible. These are natural resources which get exhausted over a period of time and cannot be readily replaced or replenished. These are exhaustible.
Examples: Wind, Sun, Water, Wood. Examples: Coal, Petroleum, Natural gas.
3. How do petroleum and natural gas occur in nature?
Answer: Petroleum and natural gas occur at moderate depths under the earth's crust, trapped between layers of impervious rocks. Natural gas, being lighter, is always found as a layer above the petroleum layer, which floats over salt water.
4. state the main advantages of using natural gas.
Answer: Natural gas is a cleaner fuel because it releases carbon dioxide in relatively smaller amounts than coal and petroleum. It burns completely without leaving behind any ash particles.
5. Keeping in mind their availability in nature, explain the need of using coal and petroleum judiciously.
Answer: Coal and petroleum are non-renewable, exhaustible resources that take millions of years to form. Since our energy needs are growing rapidly and these resources are limited, we must use them judiciously to save them for future generations and protect the environment.
