Chapter 4: Force and Pressure Solutions

A. Fill in the blanks.

1. For a force to come into play, the two concerned bodies must ____________ with each other.
Answer: interact
2. To draw water from a well, we have to ____________ the rope.
Answer: pull
3. An unbalanced force, acting on a moving object, may change its ____________ and/or its ____________.
Answer: speed and direction
4. In the game of cricket the 'bails' fall when the ball strikes them. This is an example of a/an ____________ force.
Answer: contact
5. The force responsible for raising our body hair, when we try to take off a synthetic shirt in dry weather conditions, is an ____________ force.
Answer: electrostatic
6. The atmospheric pressure ____________ as we move towards higher altitudes.
Answer: decreases

B. Write True or False for the following statements.

1. The net resultant force on an object can be zero even when several forces act on it.
Answer: True
2. A force can change the shape of an object.
Answer: True
3. Frictional force is an example of a non-contact force.
Answer: False
4. A gas filled balloon moves up because the upward force acting on it is smaller than the downward gravitational force.
Answer: False
5. We can reduce the pressure of a given force by increasing the area over which the force acts.
Answer: True
6. Inside a bottle filled with water, pressure is least at the bottom and greatest at the surface of water.
Answer: False

D. Answer the following questions in brief.

1. In the following situations, identify the agent exerting the force and the object on which the force acts and effects of force in each case:
(a) Squeezing a piece of lemon between the fingers to extract its juice.
Answer: Agent of force is fingers and object is lemon. The effect is change in shape.
(b) A person diving into a swimming pool.
Answer: Agent of force is person's muscles and object is water. The effect is change in motion.
(c) A labourer moving a loaded cart.
Answer: Agent of force is labourer and object is loaded cart. The effect is change in motion of the cart.
(d) A car coming to rest once its engine is switched off.
Answer: Agent of force is friction from the road and object is car. The effect is change in motion.
(e) An athlete making a high jump to clear a bar kept at a certain height.
Answer: Agent of force is muscles and object is athlete's body. The effect is change in motion.
2. An object experienced a net non-zero force. Is it possible for the object to move with a constant speed along a straight line?
Answer: An object experiencing a net non-zero force cannot move with a constant speed along a straight line because an unbalanced force changes the state of motion of the object.
3. Distinguish between: (a) balanced and unbalanced force (b) contact force and non-contact force.
Answer:
(a) Balanced force: The net force acting on an object is zero because the forces are equal in size and opposite in direction. The object remains stationary or continues moving uniformly.

Unbalanced force: The net force is non-zero. The forces are unequal in size or act in the same direction. The object starts moving or its motion changes.

(b) Contact force: Force acts only when two interacting bodies are in direct physical contact with each other.

Non-contact force: Force can exert its effect even from a distance without any physical contact between the bodies.
4. When we throw a ball upwards, what happens to its speed while it is going up? Give reason for your answer.
Answer: When we throw a ball upwards, its speed decreases continuously because the Earth pulls the ball downwards because of gravity (gravitational force).
5. The figure shows a man with a parachute. (a) Name the force which is responsible for his downward motion. Is it a contact force or a non-contact force? (b) Will the man come down with the same speed without the parachute? Explain.
Answer:
(a) The force responsible for his downward motion is the force of gravity. It is a non-contact force.

(b) The man will come down with a much faster speed without the parachute because the parachute has a larger surface area that creates high air resistance, which makes the speed low. Without the parachute, air resistance is lower and the person falls much faster.
6. How would the pressure, exerted by a force on an area, change if— (a) the area is doubled keeping the thrust constant? (b) thrust is doubled keeping the area constant?
Answer: Pressure = Force / Area

(a) If the area is doubled while keeping thrust constant, pressure becomes half.
(b) If thrust is doubled while keeping area constant, pressure becomes double.
7. When will a diver experience a greater pressure—100 cm below the surface of water or 200 cm below the surface of water? Justify your answer.
Answer: The diver will experience greater pressure at 200 cm below the surface.

Justification: Liquid pressure depends on the depth of the liquid column and increases with depth. Therefore, pressure is greater at 200 cm depth.

E. Answer the following questions.

1. Define force. Is it necessary for the two bodies to be in direct contact for a force to exist between them? Explain with examples.
Answer: A force is a push or pull which acts on an object due to its interaction with another object. It is not necessary for two bodies to be in direct contact for a force to exist. Some forces can act from a distance, such as gravitational force and magnetic force.
2. State three effects of force. Give suitable examples.
Answer: The three effects of force are—

1. Change the state of motion – kicking a ball.
2. Change in the direction of motion – a batsman hitting a cricket ball.
3. Change in the shape – squeezing a balloon.
3. Give reasons for the following statements.
Answer:

(a) Snow shoes are more effective than ordinary shoes for walking on snow:
Snow shoes have a large surface area which decreases the pressure on snow while walking.

(b) Porters place a large round piece of cloth on their heads when they have to carry heavy loads:
The cloth increases the contact area between the load and the head, which reduces pressure on the head.

(c) A sharp knife cuts vegetables and fruits more effectively than a blunt knife:
A sharp knife has a very small contact area, so it exerts more pressure on the vegetable. Therefore, it cuts more effectively than a blunt knife.

(d) School bags are provided with wide straps to carry them:
Wide straps provide a larger contact area and reduce the pressure on the shoulders.

(e) It is much easier to burst an inflated balloon with a needle than directly with the finger:
A needle has a very small area of contact and therefore produces more pressure on the balloon, while a finger has a larger area and exerts less pressure.

(f) Mountaineers may suffer from nose bleeding at high altitudes:
Mountaineers may suffer from nose bleeding at high altitudes because atmospheric pressure is much lower at high altitudes.

(g) Airplanes have pressurized cabins for passengers' safety while flying high in the atmosphere:
Airplanes have pressurized cabins to maintain normal atmospheric pressure at high altitudes and protect passengers from breathing problems.
4. Two rods 'A' and 'B' of the same weight and equal length have different thickness. A boy vertically places them on the surface of sand. Which one of them will sink less? Explain.
Answer: Rod B will sink less because it has a larger contact area which decreases the pressure. Rod A will sink more because it has a smaller contact area which increases the pressure.
5. Three holes A, B and C are made in an empty can at different levels one over the other as shown in figure. They are temporarily closed with adhesive tape. The can is now filled with water. What will you observe when the tape is pulled out? Justify your answer.
Answer: Water from hole C will travel the farthest distance because the liquid pressure is highest at the lowest hole. Water from hole A will travel the shortest distance because the liquid pressure is lowest there.
6. Make two tiny holes, of the same size, at equal heights from the bottom in a plastic bottle. Cover them with adhesive tape and fill the bottle with water. State the observations and conclusions based on this activity.
Answer:

Observation: Water comes out from both holes with the same force and travels the same distance because they are at the same height.

Conclusion: This activity shows that liquid pressure is exerted equally in all directions at the same depth.
7. Discuss in brief the variation of atmospheric pressure with altitude. Do changes in temperature also cause a change in atmospheric pressure?
Answer: Atmospheric pressure decreases with altitude because the air becomes thinner at higher levels. An increase in temperature also changes pressure because warm air expands and becomes lighter.
