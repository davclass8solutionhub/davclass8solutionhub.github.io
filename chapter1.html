D. Answer the following questions in brief.

1. List the factors that determine the shape of a cell.
Answer: The shape of a cell is determined by its function, its location in the body, and the pressure of surrounding cells.
2. Distinguish between unicellular and multicellular organisms. Give two examples of each.
Answer:
  • Unicellular: Amoeba, Paramecium.
  • Multicellular: Human being, Mango tree.
3. Give reasons for the following:
(a) The cell is called the structural and functional unit of life.
Answer: It is because all living organisms are physically built up of cells, and all essential life processes are carried out inside individual cells.
(b) Plant cells are more rigid than animal cells.
Answer: Because plant cells have an additional rigid outer covering called the cell wall, which is absent in animal cells.
4. Which cell organelle is known as the 'Powerhouse of the cell'? Why is it so called?
Answer: Mitochondria is called the powerhouse of the cell because it is responsible for cellular respiration and generating energy needed for different activities.
5. Name the cell organelles responsible for imparting colour to the leaves and fruits of a plant.
Answer:
  • Leaves: Chloroplasts (contain chlorophyll).
  • Fruits: Chromoplasts (contain colorful pigments).
6. What are cilia and flagella? Write one similarity and one dissimilarity between the two of them.
Answer: Cilia and flagella are tiny hair-like projections on the cell membrane that help in locomotion.
  • Similarity: Both help unicellular organisms in movement or catching food.
  • Dissimilarity: Cilia are small and many (Paramecium), flagella are longer and fewer (Euglena).
7. If onion peel cells and cheek cells are observed through a microscope, state two major differences that the observer is likely to find.
Answer: The two major differences are:
  • Cell Wall: Onion peel cells have a rigid outer cell wall, while cheek cells do not.
  • Vacuole: Onion peel cells have a large central vacuole, whereas cheek cells have very small ones or none.
8. Classify the following into cells, tissue and organ: skin, fat cell, RBC, blood, ear, muscle.
Answer:
  • Cells: Fat cell, RBC
  • Tissue: Blood, Muscle
  • Organ: Skin, Ear

E. Answer the following questions.

1. "All cells in an organism do not have the same shape." Justify the above statement by drawing at least three different cell types found in human beings.
Answer: Different cells perform different functions, so they have different shapes. For example, in human beings:
  • Red Blood Cells: Spherical or disc-shaped to carry oxygen smoothly through blood vessels.
  • Muscle Cells: Spindle-shaped to help in contraction and movement.
  • Nerve Cells: Long and branched to transmit messages over long distances.

2. Where and how are chromosomes formed? State their significance.
Answer:
  • Where and How Formed: Chromosomes are formed inside the nucleus. When a cell is ready to divide, the thread-like network called chromatin condenses to form thicker structures called chromosomes.
  • Significance: Chromosomes carry genes, which are responsible for transferring characteristics from parents to the next generation.
3. With the help of well-labelled diagrams, highlight three differences between a plant cell and an animal cell.
Answer:
Feature Plant Cell Animal Cell
Cell Wall Present (provides rigidity) Absent
Plastids Present (manufactures food) Absent
Vacuoles Large and centrally located Small and temporary
4. Write the functions performed by the following cell organelles:
(a) Endoplasmic Reticulum
Answer: It forms a network of membranes that provides channels for transport of materials within the cell. Rough ER helps in protein synthesis, and Smooth ER helps in lipid synthesis.
(b) Golgi Complex
Answer: They are sac-like structures that act as the packaging centre of the cell. They process and package materials made by the cell.
(c) Nucleus
Answer: It is the control centre of the cell. It regulates and controls all cellular activities and carries genetic material.
(d) Chloroplast
Answer: They contain green pigment (chlorophyll) and manufacture food for green plants through the process of photosynthesis.
(e) Vacuoles
Answer: They act as storage bags in the cell, storing excess water, food materials, and waste products.
(f) Mitochondria
Answer: They are the powerhouse of the cell, responsible for generating energy needed for cellular activities through cellular respiration.
5. "The cell membrane is a very important component of a cell." How is damage to the cell membrane likely to impact the functions of the cell?
Answer: The cell membrane controls the entry and exit of substances in and out of the cell. If it gets damaged, unwanted substances can freely enter the cell, and useful materials will leak out. This will disrupt all cellular activities and eventually lead to the death of the cell.
6. Define the term 'cell'. For plants/animals, Name the different organelles that make a cell. Explain why some of these is called the structural and functional unit of life.
Answer:
  • Definition: Cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms.
  • Cell Organelles: The organelles that make a cell include the Nucleus, Mitochondria, Endoplasmic Reticulum, Golgi Complex, Ribosomes, Vacuoles, and Plastids (only in plants).
  • Why Structural and Functional Unit: It is called the structural unit because all plants and animals are physically built up of cells. It is called the functional unit because all the essential life processes are carried out by individual cell.
